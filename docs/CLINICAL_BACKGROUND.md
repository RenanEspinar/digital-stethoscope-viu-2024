# Clinical scope and interpretation — VIU 2023

The original master's thesis (pp. 35–39) describes obtaining five cardiac audio recordings during exploratory evaluation at **Hospital de Clínicas, La Paz**, under medical supervision, and sending recordings to a specialist for auscultatory comments. The observations include both normal and atypical cardiac sounds, as recorded in Table 4.1 (p. 35).

**Supported conclusion:** the experimental system captured and processed sounds sufficiently for qualitative clinician review in a small exploratory setting. The manuscript does **not** provide a validated diagnostic classifier, sensitivity, specificity, reference-standard testing of every finding, or a blinded controlled comparison. A suspected rhythm anomaly in audio is not a substitute for ECG confirmation.

**Privacy:** figure 4.1 (p. 35) contains identifiable/partly identifiable people during examinations. Annexes D/E reproduce academic recording consent templates, but these do not establish explicit permission for public posting of those photos or clinical recordings. The repository deliberately excludes patient photographs and individual clinical observations.

The **2026 redesigned hardware** is a separate development; the 2023 findings do not validate it.
